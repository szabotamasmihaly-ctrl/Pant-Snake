Light-Pole Snake Kit

The printed Pant Snake, now a long coil round an ordinary street-light pole. It keeps the original head, tail, door and livery. Between them, nine identical segments wind 2.2 times round the pole, and the holders hide between the coil and the pole.

Changes from v1

What changed

Each change, with the image that shows it.

  1. A longer snake

    2.94 m and 2.2 turns round the pole. Nine identical segments between your head and tail hold 24 standard cans; v1 held 9. Two more segments make it 3.40 m.

  2. Head and tail like my printable snake

    Your own P12 head and your P01 + P02 tail, on your snake’s own coil shape. Your door, bail lever, pins, drain and hatch lids are reused unchanged.

  3. The QR code on the body below the head, not on the head

    Your panel sits on segments 7–9, just below the head. The QR centre is 1.40 m up, tilted 45° toward a person in front of it. The head keeps only its pattern.

  4. Only go round the pole, with no turn on top at the end

    The head stays on the coil. The hood of your P12 that went over the box top is trimmed off and the hat is not used, so nothing passes over the pole.

  5. Holders less visible

    No clamps or screws on the street side. Six pads sit between the coil and the pole. Four steel bands run in a groove behind the tube and show only as thin rings on the pole.

Print one snake

The files for the build and the pole above. Before printing the rest, tilt one printed segment to 24.8° and check that a can slides: can-on-PETG friction has no published value, and the slope leaves little margin.

9 segments · 6 pads · 6.9 kg of PETG

About 95–120 printer-hours for the body (estimate from 5.2 litres of plastic, slice to confirm). The painted beds in 3mf/ carry the livery for the 9-segment build; beds for 11 segments are in 3mf/11-segment-build/.

Hardware for one snake: 4 stainless bands 19 × 0.76 mm with buckles (about 0.5 m each), 4 strips of 1 mm EPDM under the bands, 6 self-adhesive 3 mm EPDM liners cut 58 × 70 mm, a few 1 mm EPDM shims, neutral-cure silicone and the glue you used for P01 and P02. The README has the full list.

    Checked numbers

    Physics and the rules of the street

    The full working is in physics.md beside the files. Values marked as assumed or estimated were not measured or quoted.

    QuestionAnswer
    Will a can slide?Yes, if friction μ is below 0.46 (24.8° slope, round bore). Lacquered aluminium on PETG is about 0.2–0.35 dry (assumed, no published value). A sticky can can stop; the next can pushes it on.
    How fast does it arrive?At most 1.8 m/s at the tail, for a slippery can. The side force in the bend limits the speed.
    How hard does it land?Like a 16 cm drop. Only the first can hits your tail floor; the rest land on cans.
    Can it jam?No. Every can and 0.5 l bottle passes every bend with at least 18 mm to spare, and a tilted can would need μ of 0.6 or more to wedge. Two slim cans side by side still pass.
    What does it hold?24 × 33 cl cans, 17 × 50 cl cans or 13 × 0.5 l PET bottles in the standard build.
    Is it strong enough?Safety factor 1.6–2.8 against a 12 MPa design stress across the print layers, for a 1 kN kick on the mouth, 1.5 kN of someone sitting on the head and 2 kN on the coil.
    Will it slip down the pole?Four bands hold 3.2 kN on bare steel and 4.7 kN on a 1 mm EPDM strip, after liner creep, against 1.65 kN of snake, full cans and misuse: safety factor 2.0 and 2.8. Use the strip.
    Will it turn round the pole?Not from wind or cans. A deliberate shove of about 0.2 kN (thinnest pole) to 0.45 kN (thickest) sideways on the mouth turns it the weaker way; it then hangs on the top pad and cannot fall. Turn it back and re-tension.
    Does it overload the pole?Wind on the snake uses 3.7–7.9 % of a 4–6 m column's capacity. The pole owner still signs off an EN 40-3-3 check.

    Before you mount one

    • Aarhus bans all items on its light masts (belysningsstrategi del 3). A pilot there needs a campus or private pole, or a written exception from Aarhus Kommune, Trafik: lys@aarhus.dk.
    • Copenhagen accepts fittings only for a public purpose, approved by both EDF and TMF Trafik, with no holes or damage.
    • Prague has a formal route for non-advertising add-ons fixed with a clamp or strap (THMP standard).
    • Leave passive-safety (crash-friendly) poles alone everywhere.
    • The pole's service door must stay free. The lowest band is at 0.86 m. The door fits best almost straight below the head, about 50° round from where the panel faces, where its top may reach 0.74 m; image 09 shows the side.

    Still open

    • Mouth height. 1.67 m suits most adults but is above a wheelchair user's side reach of about 1.22 m. The 7-segment build lowers it to 1.48 m.
    • Pole size. v2 fits Ø64–132 mm only; thicker poles would need a wider coil and a refit of your head and tail.
    • Band creep on the EPDM has a 50 % reserve but is untested. Re-tension after the first month and at the yearly check.
    • PETG outdoors yellows and turns brittle in a few years of UV and frost; print a long-term snake in ASA or give it a clear UV coat.
