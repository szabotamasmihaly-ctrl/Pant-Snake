What it can take

I slid rigid models of common drink containers through the coil of the 120 mm body. The new STL adds two screw-in emergency hatches on the underside, about a third and two thirds of the way along, and a bail-latched door at the tail. The hatches are in the clear window band. The table below was measured on the previous body: the tube follows the same path, but I have not re-run the sweep on the new file. The tube is about 120 mm inside with a 3 mm wall, and its bends are gentle (radius 155–350 mm, median 184 mm). Every can size, half-litre plastic bottles and the usual glass beer bottles now pass. Litre bottles and wine bottles still do not. The info panel by the neck says what goes in (cans and bottles up to 0.5 l) and what doesn’t (big bottles, rubbish): "PANT HER" in Denmark, "DOBOZ + PALACK" in Hungary.

Clearance is the smallest gap between container and inner wall at the tightest point of the coil, with the container free to shift inside the tube; negative means it collides. Containers run in single file, since two 66 mm cans side by side need 132 mm. The snake holds about 21 cans (33 cl), 14 tall cans, 11 half-litre PET bottles or 10 glass beer bottles. When full that is DKK 21 in cans or about DKK 10–16 in bottles in Denmark, and roughly 500–1,050 Ft in Hungary. Containers go in at the neck and come out of the bail-latched door at the tail; the head opening (not in these files) needs at least about 75 mm clear. Glass now fits, but the first bottle into an empty snake slides about 1.6 m down the coil onto the door. The spiral slows it, and the door is 12 mm thick, but test with a few bottles before allowing glass, or add a rubber pad on the door.

Glow, window and outdoor durability

Yes, phosphorescent filament works here. Every design is print-only, with no painting. Each print section uses at most five materials: three weather-proof colours, one glow filament and one clear filament. That fits a 5-head toolchanger exactly, and colour changes waste almost no filament.

Body colours · ASA

e.g. Prusament ASA ≈ €33–35/kg

  • UV-stable, made for outdoor use; colours hold for years in Danish weather.
  • Needs an enclosed printer (warps, styrene fumes).

Glow details · glow ASA

Spectrum ASA 275 Glow ≈ €50/kg · or Prusament PETG Ultraglow €87.50/kg

  • Strontium aluminate charges in daylight and under white LED street lights. It is brightest for the first 20–40 min after dark and still visible for hours (Ultraglow: 6–8 h in full darkness).
  • Looks pale in daylight: cream (green glow), pale aqua, pale blue, pale lilac (violet) or pale peach (orange). Every design uses that daylight colour too. Green, aqua and blue glow longest; violet and orange are dimmer, so the QR codes only use green, aqua or blue.
  • Very abrasive: use hardened 0.6 mm nozzles.
  • Glow ASA bonds with the ASA body. Ultraglow is brighter but PETG does not bond well to ASA.
  • Don't put a UV-blocking clear coat over glow parts; it stops them charging.

Fill window · clear PC

transparent polycarbonate ≈ €35–40/kg (or clear PETG €33/kg)

  • A 37 mm strip on the lower street side, where the cans rest, with glowing marks every 5 cans.
  • Printed clear plastic is see-through enough to show can colours and the fill line, but it isn't glass-clear. Print it at 100% infill, hot and slow, and clear-coat the outer face.
  • Give the window a UV clear coat; PC yellows without one. Print a small ASA + PC test piece first to check that the two materials bond.

Print-only rules

applied to every design

  • Exactly five materials per snake: three colours, one glow filament (green or blue) and clear. Nothing changes between sections.
  • The info panel by the neck uses the darkest of the three colours, so it needs no extra material. Print that section with a 0.4 mm hardened nozzle so the 1.1 mm QR modules come out sharp.
  • No line, dot or letter stroke thinner than 2 mm, about three passes of a 0.6 mm nozzle. The QR code is the one exception: its modules are 1.1 mm.
  • Colour only needs to go about 1.2 mm deep, as a skin over the base colour. The clear window runs through the full 3 mm wall so you can see inside.
  • The slicer's multi-material painting (or one STL per colour, exported from this layout) assigns each colour to its tool.

Cost per snake

Mass comes from your STL: the 3 mm wall of the 120 mm body, with tail and neck cap, is 5,209 cm³, plus 294 cm³ for the bail door and latch and 27 cm³ for the two hatches, so 5,530 cm³ in total. The pole and signal box are not printed. Each design's split between body colours, glow and window is measured from its own pattern. All inputs can be edited; prices include VAT, and EUR is converted at 7.46 DKK.

Glow filament costs about 50% more than ASA (Ultraglow about 2.5×), so the designs with the most phosphor cost the most; the table shows the glow share and price of each one. Most of the cost is still the roughly 250 hours of printing. If you print yourself, set labour to 0. The defaults are Danish prices; for Budapest, lower the electricity and labour rates to local levels.
